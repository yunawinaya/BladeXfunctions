## 2. The seven conflicts (your section 4.2)

| Ref | Answer |
|---|---|
| **C1** Queued failure | Corrected. A background completion records the status it started from (Draft or Created). It puts the IA back to that status, with its holds untouched, **only** when the stock engine applied nothing: a refusal at planning, revalidation or the batch step, an issue refused with a result code, or an issue the engine never started (no intent recorded after 8 waits). A server error, recorded intents without an answer, the same operation still running after the waits, or any failure after the components were issued (the receipt, the finish) ends the job **Requires Review** and leaves the IA Processing: nothing is reset and nothing is posted again. RELEASE_DECISION used to say "back to Created" without this condition; it is corrected. |
| **C2** Completion crash | The earlier sentence "the IA is then already Completed, so no retry is needed" is withdrawn. The classic completion is not atomic: it writes the header Completed, then issues each component, then receives the output. Since 17:07 the completion's claim records how it ended: DONE; PARTIAL\|SUBTRACT (an issue was refused after the header was written); PARTIAL\|RECEIPT (the receipt was refused); V2 (a background completion, whose job owns the effects and writes Completed only after them); LOST (a refused attempt). A claim still HELD after 10 minutes means the run stopped. IA_HOLD CHECK reads the latest claim that did not lose, and the IA form shows a warning on such an IA: "This completion stopped while issuing its components ... Needs review: check this Item Assembly's stock movements before correcting it." Recovery is a person's: the IA's movements (transaction type IA, the IA number) show what was issued and received; nothing is repaired or issued again automatically. The 10-minute lease only lets a new attempt start; it does not prove that the earlier one stopped. An attempt that stalls more than 10 minutes between its claim and its header write could still write after a newer attempt: the classic save has no fencing for that window. Revert and complete again after a partial completion is **not verified**. A new IA completed in one step takes no claim (no other completion can race it), so it has no record and CHECK says nothing. |
| **C3** Delete ordering | One sequence, the IA list's (v27). (1) Re-read the stored status: only Draft and Cancelled continue. (2) Soft-delete the header; IA_HOLD then refuses any new reservation for it. (3) IA_HOLD RELEASE_ALL, retried 3 times on a busy answer. (4) Only on 200 with nothing held: soft-delete the lines. Otherwise the header is restored and the delete refused. The durable intermediate owner is the deleted header itself: it keeps its hold mark until the release sets it to none. An interruption after step 2 (a closed browser, a lost answer, a failed restore) is found by the list's sweep (deleted IAs still marked, up to 20 at a time) and released when anyone opens the list (every 10 minutes per browser). RELEASE_DECISION's "releases first" wording is corrected. The platform's soft delete is unchanged. Evidence: automated; the live sweep found nothing to recover; an interrupted delete was not staged live. |
| **C4** Status-based release | IA_HOLD never releases by the list's or the caller's view. Each run takes the IA's run lock (`IAH\|<ia>` ticket), then re-reads the IA's stored status and its own hold rows (doc_id = this IA) under it, and decides from those. Created: hold what the saved lines need and release only this IA's surplus. Draft: give back what this IA still holds; by policy a Draft holds nothing, since a revert ends Draft with nothing held and a Created IA never goes back to Draft. Cancelled: release. Completed: close the current cycle only (after the last revert, net of that cycle's delivered rows). A stale request, for example a Draft release that arrives after the IA was saved as Created again, is decided by the status read under the lock: it reserves, it does not release. Historical Draft holds: none exist (checked 12:06: on DEV every IA hold row was finished; on PROD there are no IA hold rows). Not covered: hold rows carry no cycle id; the cycle is the status plus the revert boundary. |
| **C5** Feature off | Done (IA_HOLD v11). IA_HOLD has its own switch, `HD_ACQUIRE_ENABLED` (ON). Off, no run makes a new hold, whoever calls: the form, the save's follow-up, Retry Reservation or a direct request. Surplus release, close after completion, Cancel, Delete's release and the sweep work as before, and the answer says "Reservation is switched off". Turning the form and save switch `RES_ENABLED` off as well hides the Created button and records no new Reserving. Off is proven by automated tests only: it ships ON. |
| **C6** Cancellation | Authorization: the product owner decided it on 22 October in this project: "to releases the reservation it should be Cancel tho with 'Cancelled' status, same way like GD logic" and "Delete is for Deleting Draft and Cancelled". **Before:** a Created IA could be deleted (release, then delete), and no Cancelled IA existed. **After:** Created → Cancel → Delete. Cancel releases everything first and only then sets Cancelled; if the release cannot finish, the IA stays Created. Delete is for Draft and Cancelled only. Impacts: the list refuses to delete a Created IA ("cancel it first"); a Cancelled IA is read-only (the server refuses saving, completing and reserving it); the SO line summary counts a Cancelled IA as neither open nor produced; mobile has no Cancel button, and its delete is the MIN-04 gap. Existing records: Cancelled is a new status, so there are none on PROD to preserve. It ships ON tonight under the same decision. |
| **C7** Scope and dates | Section 1 is the one map. RELEASE_DECISION and BA_TECH_MAP are corrected to it. The release itself is the product owner's decision (RELEASE_DECISION section 1), taken with the Blocked, Unmet and Partial items above open. This reply does not claim that they are protected. |
